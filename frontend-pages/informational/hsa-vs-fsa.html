HSA vs FSA: Understanding the Differences

Both HSAs (Health Savings Accounts) and FSAs (Flexible Spending Accounts) are tax-advantaged accounts for healthcare expenses. Understand the key differences between HSA vs FSA to make the best choice for your situation.

HSA vs FSA Overview

HSAs and FSAs are both valuable tax-advantaged healthcare savings tools, but they have significant differences that affect how you can use them. Understanding HSA vs FSA will help you maximize your healthcare savings.

While both allow pre-tax contributions for healthcare expenses, they differ in eligibility, contribution limits, rollover rules, and portability. Here's a comprehensive HSA vs FSA comparison.

Key Differences: HSA vs FSA

Eligibility

HSA: You must be enrolled in a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) to be eligible for an HSA account.

FSA: You can have a Flexible Spending Account regardless of your health plan type. FSAs are more widely available through employers.

Contribution Limits

HSA: For 2026, contribution limits are $4,150 (individual) or $8,300 (family), plus an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution for those 55+.

FSA: For 2026, the contribution limit is $3,300 annually with no catch-up contributions allowed.

Rollover and "Use-It-or-Lose-It"

HSA: Unused funds roll over indefinitely. There is NO "use-it-or-lose-it" rule, making HSAs ideal for long-term health savings.

FSA: Uses the "use-it-or-lose-it" rule. Unused funds are forfeited at year-end, though employers may offer a $610 carryover or grace period.

Portability

HSA: Your HSA account is portable and remains yours when you change jobs or health plans. You own the account completely.

FSA: Your FSA account is typically tied to your employer and ends when you leave your job.

Investment Options

HSA: Many HSAs offer investment options, allowing your balance to grow over time. You can earn interest or invest in mutual funds.

FSA: FSAs typically only accrue interest and don't offer investment options. Funds are held in a non-interest bearing account.

Eligible Expenses

HSA: Covers HSA eligible expenses including medical, dental, vision, and mental health services. Can also be used for certain over-the-counter items.

FSA: Covers FSA eligible expenses which are similar to HSA but with some differences. Dependent care FSA is a separate account for childcare.

Distributions

HSA: You can withdraw funds anytime without penalties for eligible expenses. At age 65, non-medical withdrawals are allowed (subject to income tax).

FSA: You can only access funds that align with your claim submissions during the plan year. Distributions must match your submitted expenses.

HSA vs HRA: Additional Comparison

There's also the Health Reimbursement Arrangement (HRA), which is yet another type of healthcare account. Unlike HSA vs FSA comparisons where the accounts are owned by employees, HRAs are employer-owned accounts. The key difference in HSA vs HRA is that HRAs don't require HDHP enrollment and are funded entirely by the employer.

Can You Have HSA and FSA at the Same Time?

Generally, you cannot have both a regular HSA and a medical FSA simultaneously. However, there are exceptions: You can have an HSA AND a dependent care FSA at the same time. Dependent care FSA is specifically for childcare expenses and doesn't conflict with HSA eligibility.

Which is Right for You: HSA or FSA?

Choose an HSA if you:

  • Have or can switch to a High Deductible Health Plan
  • Want to build long-term health savings
  • Prefer account portability when changing jobs
  • Want investment growth potential
  • Don't need to use all funds each year

Choose an FSA if you:

  • Have predictable healthcare expenses each year
  • Can't switch to a HDHP
  • Plan to spend all contributed funds annually
  • Want employer contributions to your account
  • Prefer higher contribution limits in specific situations
