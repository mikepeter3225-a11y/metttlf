How Does a Health Savings Account (HSA) Work?

Understanding how a health savings account works is essential for maximizing your healthcare savings. Learn the mechanics of HSA, including contributions, withdrawals, and tax benefits.

The Basics: How Does HSA Work?

A Health Savings Account (HSA) works by allowing you to contribute pre-tax dollars that can be used for healthcare expenses. It's a personal savings account that you own completely, which differs from employer-sponsored plans like FSAs.

The fundamental premise is simple: contribute pre-tax money, use it for eligible healthcare expenses, and receive tax benefits at every step of the process.

Step-by-Step: How HSA Works

Step 1: Verify HDHP Eligibility

To open an HSA, you must be enrolled in a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). This is the first requirement for HSA eligibility.

Step 2: Open Your HSA Account

Once eligible, you can open an HSA through your employer (if offered) or independently through a financial institution. Opening an HSA is straightforward - provide basic information and set up your account.

Step 3: Make Contributions

You can contribute to your HSA through payroll deduction (pre-tax) or direct contributions. The contributions reduce your taxable income, saving you money on taxes immediately.

Step 4: Use or Save Funds

You have two options with your HSA funds:

  • Use Immediately: Pay for current healthcare expenses with your HSA balance
  • Save for Later: Let your balance grow, rolling over year to year with no limit

Step 5: Claim Eligible Expenses

When you incur qualified healthcare expenses, you can use your HSA debit card or request reimbursement from your HSA balance.

Step 6: Keep Documentation

Maintain receipts and documentation of all healthcare expenses for tax purposes and substantiation if requested by the IRS.

HSA Contributions: How They Work

HSA contributions come in two main forms:

Employer Contributions

  • Made through payroll with pre-tax dollars
  • Employer contributions don't count toward your personal contribution limit
  • Reduce your taxable income further
  • No FICA taxes on employer contributions

Personal Contributions

  • Made with pre-tax dollars (if through payroll) or post-tax (if direct contribution)
  • Count toward annual contribution limits
  • Can be deducted on your tax return if made with post-tax dollars
  • For 2026: $4,150 (individual) or $8,300 (family)

Catch-up Contributions

  • Available at age 55 and older
  • Additional $1,000 per year allowed
  • Increases your total HSA savings potential

HSA Withdrawals: How They Work

HSA withdrawals operate differently depending on whether they're for eligible expenses:

For Eligible Healthcare Expenses

  • Withdraw anytime, tax-free
  • No income taxes or penalties
  • No limit on withdrawal frequency
  • Funds must be used for qualified medical expenses

For Non-Medical Expenses

  • Subject to income tax at your ordinary tax rate
  • Plus 20% penalty if under age 65
  • After age 65, only income tax applies (penalty waived)
  • At 65+, HSA becomes more like a traditional IRA

How to Request HSA Withdrawals

  • Use your HSA debit card for direct payments
  • Request reimbursement online or by mail
  • Contact customer service for withdrawal assistance

The Triple Tax Advantage: How It Works

Understanding the triple tax advantage is key to how HSA works so effectively:

Tax Advantage #1: Tax-Deductible Contributions

Contributions reduce your taxable income dollar-for-dollar. If you contribute $1,000 to your HSA, your taxable income decreases by $1,000, saving you taxes at your marginal tax rate.

Tax Advantage #2: Tax-Free Growth

Any earnings on your HSA balance through interest or investments grow tax-free. You don't pay taxes on interest or investment gains.

Tax Advantage #3: Tax-Free Withdrawals

Withdrawals for eligible healthcare expenses are tax-free. You don't pay income tax on the money you withdraw for qualified medical expenses.

HSA Portability: How It Works

One of the key ways HSA works in your favor is portability:

  • Your HSA account belongs to you, not your employer
  • When you change jobs, your HSA comes with you
  • You can continue using your HSA balance indefinitely
  • You can transfer your HSA to another provider
  • Your account is never forfeited when employment ends

This portability is a major advantage of how HSA works compared to employer-sponsored FSA accounts.

Investment Options: Growing Your HSA

Many HSA providers, including MetLife, offer investment options so you can grow your HSA balance beyond basic savings:

  • High-yield savings accounts
  • Money market funds
  • Mutual funds
  • Individual stocks (with some providers)
  • Low-cost ETFs

Once your HSA reaches a certain balance (typically $1,000-$2,000), you can move funds into investments to potentially grow your long-term health savings.

Rollover Rules: How Unused Funds Work

Unlike FSAs with "use-it-or-lose-it" rules, HSA funds roll over indefinitely:

  • No annual limit on carried-over balances
  • Unused funds remain in your account year after year
  • You can accumulate significant health savings over time
  • Make withdrawals whenever you need them

This is how HSA works as a long-term health savings and investment vehicle, not just a current-year spending account.

HSA After Age 65: How Rules Change

Once you turn 65, how HSA works changes somewhat:

  • You can still use funds for healthcare expenses tax-free
  • Non-medical withdrawals are allowed without penalty
  • Non-medical withdrawals are subject to income tax only
  • Your HSA functions similarly to a Traditional IRA

Many people use this feature to essentially "unlock" their HSA for retirement planning.
